Ghost mode is an addition requested for OCS ’27. The spec describes meeting rooms, host controls, and interview authorization, but does not name ghost observers. Local OCS code gives Convex authority over a booking-specific grant and makes its own SFU hide the observer from the applicant roster and count while blocking outgoing media, chat, reactions, and hand controls. The applicant page tells candidates that authorized panel members may observe unseen.

This is reviewable code in draft OCS-27 PR #24, not a deployed integration. Backend tests, web TypeScript checks, and both web production builds pass locally. A native-worker SFU socket test verifies waiting, admission, ghost visibility and blocked actions; a two-browser media call and network deployment have not been verified. A revoked observer loses access on the next capability renewal; an uncooperative connected client is disconnected when its current pass expires, at most two minutes later. Room state is in one SFU process until shared coordination is designed.

What last year’s portal did

The 2025–26 Explore ACM-VIT portal linked a booked scheduler slot to its in-app Meets client and minted an authenticated, expiring SFU join token. Its own mediasoup/Socket.IO SFU owned the meeting. Its admin-only Ghost Mode hid ghost participants from ordinary roster snapshots and locked their mic, camera, screen share, and hand controls; server handlers also rejected chat and reactions.

Old scheduler join flow · Meeting opens in the old portal · Authenticated SFU token · Ghost toggle · Server-side ghost/admin check · Roster filtering · Ghost chat restriction · Attendance and operator actions

For 2027, reuse the old user experience and server-side restrictions within OCS-27 itself: Convex authorizes the booking and the first-party SFU enforces the hidden observer role.

Rules that should survive implementation

Backend owns decisions

Derive identity from the authenticated session. Enforce roles, eligibility, validation, booking capacity, and progression in backend functions.

Protect applicant results

Keep unannounced decisions out of applicant responses, counts, routes, and errors. Expose a safe server-side projection.

Keep retries safe

Enrollment, form submission, slot booking, task assignment, and transcript ingestion need idempotency and concurrency protection.

Keep service authority clear

OCS sfu/ runs meetings, Convex governs selection, Judge0 would execute code, and AI would give evidence-linked advice without changing applicant state.

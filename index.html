Ghost mode is an addition requested for OCS ’27. The spec describes Conclave rooms, host controls, and interview authorization, but does not name ghost observers. OCS can authorize an observer; Conclave must enforce receive-only media, roster/count filtering, hidden join/leave events, and staff audit visibility. The applicant should be told that authorized panel members may observe, even when the live roster does not show them.

The spec sets a configurable join window, defaulting to 15 minutes before the slot. OCS stores room and meeting identifiers plus authorization metadata; it does not copy Conclave media-session state.

What last year’s portal did

The 2025–26 Explore ACM-VIT portal linked a booked scheduler slot to its in-app Meets client and minted an authenticated, expiring SFU join token. Its own mediasoup/Socket.IO SFU—not the 2027 Conclave service—owned the meeting. Its admin-only Ghost Mode hid ghost participants from ordinary roster snapshots and locked their mic, camera, screen share, and hand controls; server handlers also rejected chat and reactions.

Old scheduler join flow · Meeting opens in the old portal · Authenticated SFU token · Ghost toggle · Server-side ghost/admin check · Roster filtering · Ghost chat restriction · Attendance and operator actions

For 2027, reuse the old user experience and its server-side restrictions, but put room access behind OCS booking authorization and implement the hidden observer role inside Conclave.

Trust rule: applicant identity comes from the authenticated Convex session, never a client-supplied email or user ID. Convex functions enforce authorization, ownership, validation, concurrency, and state transitions for every request.

8. Data groups and what each one owns

The target Convex schema keeps workflow data in OCS and service-owned runtime state in its own service. Convex is authoritative for applicant selection, booking, tasks, review, and transcript access. Conclave owns live media-session state; Judge0 owns execution.

Rules that should survive implementation

Backend owns decisions

Derive identity from the authenticated session. Enforce roles, eligibility, validation, booking capacity, and progression in backend functions.

Protect applicant results

Keep unannounced decisions out of applicant responses, counts, routes, and errors. Expose a safe server-side projection.

Keep retries safe

Enrollment, form submission, slot booking, task assignment, and transcript ingestion need idempotency and concurrency protection.

Keep service authority clear

Conclave runs meetings, Judge0 executes code, OCS governs selection, and AI gives evidence-linked advice without changing applicant state.
